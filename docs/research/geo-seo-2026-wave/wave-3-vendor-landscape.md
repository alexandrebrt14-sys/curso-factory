## 1. spatie/schema-org

**GitHub:** https://github.com/spatie/schema-org  
**License:** MIT  
**Approx. stars:** ~2.5k  
**Last commit:** 2026  

spatie/schema-org is a PHP library that lets you fluently build Schema.org entities (e.g., `Article`, `Course`, `Product`) and export them as JSON‑LD, which makes it straightforward to standardize GEO‑relevant markup across a large content portfolio. GEO teams use it to ensure consistent structured data that AI crawlers can interpret, and to rapidly experiment with entity properties (e.g., `about`, `mentions`, `author`, `courseCode`) aligned with how LLMs extract knowledge.

## 2. schema-org-mcp

**GitHub:** https://github.com/modelcontextprotocol/servers/tree/main/schema-org  
**License:** MIT  
**Approx. stars:** <100 (subdirectory in the MCP servers repo)  
**Last commit:** 2025

### On llms.txt Validators / Generators and AI Citation Trackers

As of 2025–2026, there are **no well‑established open‑source llms.txt validator/generator projects on GitHub** that are comparable to the proprietary tools run by mrs.digital or geordy.ai; most implementations appear to be small scripts or closed‑source SaaS utilities. Likewise, dedicated “AI citation crawler/visibility trackers” like Airefs, Otterly, Profound, and AthenaHQ are commercial products, not open‑source GitHub projects. For open GEO stacks today, teams typically combine:

- Open‑source crawlers/extractors (crawl4ai, scrapy, trafilatura, newspaper3k);
- Structured‑data tooling (spatie/schema-org, extruct, pyld, schema-org-mcp);
- Search/LLM frameworks (Meilisearch, Typesense, llamaindex, LangChain);

and then glue them together with custom scripts or MCP servers modeled after `openai-mcp-server` for internal, GEO‑oriented analytics and optimization.
